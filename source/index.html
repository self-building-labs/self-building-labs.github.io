A lab that builds
the capabilities science needs.

Self-Building Labs build and retain the controls, skills, workflows, and layouts that new experiments need.

  1. Identify what is missing

    Find the controls, operations, and procedures the lab needs.

  2. Build the capability

    Build digital twins, interfaces, and robot skills. Simulate operations before approved physical trials.

  3. Validate and reuse

    Refine operations with physical feedback. Save successful skills with clear reuse conditions.

How SBL changes laboratory automation.

Self-driving labs run and optimize experiments using existing capabilities. SBL develops new ones, starting from a manual lab or SDL.

CapabilityTraditional labSelf-driving labSelf-building lab
Running experimentsResearcher-led proceduresAutomation with existing capabilitiesWorkflows with capabilities built as needed
Adding capabilitiesResearcher developmentUsually specialist engineeringAgent development and validation; human oversight
Retaining knowledgeProcedures and experienceSetup-specific protocols and scriptsReusable skills with validation and use conditions

What self-building labs will unlock in the future.

The goal is lasting capabilities that other labs can reuse.

Less rebuilding

Test whether reusing validated skills improves performance and reduces engineering effort and human intervention.

Skills shared across labs

Share twins, controls, skills, records, and validation conditions. A capability map could match goals to equipment and gaps. Check transfers locally.

Science with limited support

Build on available equipment in field stations, hazardous sites, or space, despite limited resources, communication, and support.
